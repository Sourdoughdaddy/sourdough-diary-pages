It starts with flour and water: how Sourdough Diary began

There is a moment everyone knows who has ever started a sourdough: you stand over a jar, look in, and wonder whether it is alive or already not any more. For me that was January. The starter sort of worked. The bread after it did not. Nor did the next one, and the one after that least of all.

None of my friends bake sourdough. So there was nobody to ask why flour, water and patience flatly refused to become bread. I went where you go with questions like that: the internet. I read and read, and watched YouTube videos for hours.

Two months of only the jar

At some point I stopped baking altogether. For two months I looked after nothing but the starter: different feeding ratios, different flours, and for a while five jars in the fridge at once.

My first starter was called Breadniss. Rest in peace. She was a good one.

In March I dared to try a loaf again. It sort of worked, and at that point "sort of" was a triumph.

Nobody had warned me

Sourdough is flour, water and salt, plus roughly a thousand details nobody prepares you for. Had I known how hard it is without proper guidance, I would probably never have started.

But loaf by loaf it got better, and the joy rose along with the bread. I talked about it non-stop, made it my whole personality, and fell down a rabbit hole I still haven't found my way out of. To be fair, I'm not looking very hard.

A million questions

Eventually my colleague wanted to bake too. Conveniently, she is also my best friend at work. I gave her a piece of my starter and explained how to begin. She was thrilled and got going at once.

And of course she had a million questions. Just like me in January, with one decisive difference: she had someone to text. She did, constantly, and I answered all of it. Patiently, I would like to point out.

Until one day I said: you know what, I'll quickly program an app that walks you through it, so you don't forget anything.

"Quickly" was, looking back, the wrong word. The idea was still the right one.

Why the jar has opinions

Because sourdough has personality. It is alive, through and through, and anything alive naturally has a loud opinion on everything. Just like me.

Sourdough Diary is the guidance I didn't have in January: when the starter is ready, what comes next and for how long, and what went wrong with the last loaf. And the piece of starter you hand on is in there too, as the family tree.

The best time to start with sourdough, by the way, would have been with someone to ask. The second best is now, with a jar that talks back.
