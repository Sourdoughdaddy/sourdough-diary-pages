How Sourdough Diary started

In January I tried to make a sourdough starter. It sort of worked. Then I tried to bake with it, and failed. Miserably, and many times.

None of my friends bake sourdough, so there was nobody to ask. I went to the internet instead. I read and read, and watched YouTube videos for hours.

Two months of only the jar

Then I stopped baking altogether. For two months I did nothing but try to get the starter right. Different feeding ratios, different flours. At one point there were five jars in my fridge at the same time.

My first starter was called Breadniss. Rest in peace. She was a good one.

In March I tried a loaf again, and it sort of worked.

Nobody had warned me

Sourdough has so many details that nobody prepared me for. If I had known how hard it is without proper guidance, I probably would never have started.

But it got better, loaf after loaf, and I started to enjoy it. Then I loved it. I talked about it non-stop and made it my whole personality. It was a massive rabbit hole and I went all the way down.

A million questions

Eventually my colleague, who is also my best friend at work, wanted to start baking too. I gave her a bit of my starter and told her how to begin. She was enthusiastic and got to work right away.

And of course she had a million questions, just like I did when I started. The difference was that she had someone to ask. She texted me all the time, and I answered everything, patiently.

Then one day I said: you know what, I'll quickly program an app that guides you through it, so you won't forget anything.

That was the idea. It was not quick.

Why the jar has opinions

Because sourdough has so much personality. It is fully alive. So obviously it should have loud opinions on everything. Just like me.

Sourdough Diary is the guidance I didn't have in January: when the starter is ready, what to do next and for how long, and what went wrong with the last loaf. And giving someone a piece of your starter is in there too, as the family tree.
